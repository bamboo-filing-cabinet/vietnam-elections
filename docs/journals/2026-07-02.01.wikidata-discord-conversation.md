**thviet:** Hey guys, I have this project where I collect and archive Vietnam election data — https://github.com/bamboo-filing-cabinet/vietnam-elections/ — with a UI [here](https://bamboo-filing-cabinet.github.io/vietnam-elections/). Having learnt about Wikidata for the second time from the meetup yesterday, I was wondering how election data is best collected/archived. My conversation with AI says this about Wikidata (slightly shortened):

> **Q:** So in summary, how good is Wikidata for election data archiving and collection?
>
> **A:** The two verbs in your question pull apart cleanly.
>
> For **collection** (the working store where data is gathered and structured), it's good for one layer and bad for the other. The relational scaffolding — who ran, who won, which office, which term, who succeeded whom — fits its graph model well and is a mature part of Wikidata. The quantitative returns don't: vote counts per candidate per unit are n-ary facts with no natural main value, so each becomes a reified statement-plus-qualifiers that's heavy to write and painful to aggregate. Your case is harder still, since Wikidata's electoral model assumes competitive single-winner democracies, while you have multi-member units, derived winners, and a single-party vetting context it handles awkwardly.
>
> For **archiving**, it's the wrong tool, and that's the sharper point. Wikidata is a living, mutably-edited knowledge base — statements change, items merge or vanish — whereas an archive's job is the opposite: fixed, dated, source-faithful, preserved. Your repo already does this better, keeping the original source document, a fetched date, a rights posture, and the raw number formatting, and treating "won" as a dated, sourced claim rather than a mutable fact.

I assume Wikidata has encountered this problem space before — I wonder if anyone has already delved into it and can share their opinion.

**Clair:** Hey! Sorry for taking so long to respond — @Pharos (Richard) let me know you have a question about longitudinal data in Wikidata.

Unfortunately there isn't a good answer that is able to be queried via the Wikidata Query Service right now. Most Wikidata editors prefer to only keep the most up-to-date/recent (or occasionally maybe a few instances going backward) info for a given statement. But this can be highly variable and there is no site-wide policy (see [NYC, head of government](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q60) for example).

However, you are in luck for election data specifically — there is a Wikidata WikiProject for elections here: https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:WikiProject_elections. Usually each election gets its own Wikidata item, avoiding the whole most-recent/archive-everything dichotomy, in favor of individual pages for each individual election. Not everything is answered by the WikiProject, but the users at the end of the page should have a good idea of how to get you started!
